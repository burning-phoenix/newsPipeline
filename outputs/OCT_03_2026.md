## Flydubai Co-Pilot's Attack Forces Emergency Landing in Saudi Arabia

A Flydubai pilot attacked his flight captain with a crash ax and tried to take control of the aircraft's controls in what a United Arab Emirates official described Saturday as an attempted terrorist attack, on a flight from Dubai to Tel Aviv (South China Morning Post, Deutsche Welle, BBC News and others). Passengers overpowered the co-pilot, and two off-duty pilots who were on the flight managed to gain control of the aircraft and make an emergency landing in Tabuk, Saudi Arabia (Deutsche Welle, BBC News, CBS News and others). Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said in a video statement that the co-pilot had "undergone Islamist radical indoctrination" and intended to crash the jet (Euronews, NBC News, CBS News and others). Media outlets have named the co-pilot as Hammam al-Hammami, an Omani national (The Guardian, CBS News, BBC News and others), and CNN, citing two sources familiar with the matter, reported he was removed from a job at Oman's national airline last year over concerns about extremist views (Deutsche Welle, BBC News, NBC News and others). How the co-pilot came to be working in the cockpit on the sensitive route to Israel is a central question for investigators (South China Morning Post, BBC News, Euronews and others).

CBS News emphasized the co-pilot's background and radicalization, quoting Netanyahu's framing. NBC News led with the UAE official's "terrorist attack" statement and included emotional reactions from passengers and the pilot. The New York Times highlighted the heroism of the pilot and passengers while raising questions about vetting, and The Guardian led with the crash ax detail and questions about how the weapon was obtained. South China Morning Post focused on the co-pilot being barred from flying and the resulting security questions, while BBC News reported the UAE statement alongside a timeline and passenger accounts.

The accounts disagreed on the captain's whereabouts after treatment: NBC News reported he spoke from a hospital in Abu Dhabi, while Euronews reported a video call from his hospital bed in Saudi Arabia. They also disagreed on whether the co-pilot had been banned from flying, as NBC News reported citing The Wall Street Journal, or removed from flight training and given another role, as BBC News reported. Estimates of the number of people on board ranged from 174, according to CBS News, to 182, according to Euronews, Deutsche Welle, The Guardian and South China Morning Post.

## Tennessee Prisons Chief to Resign After Failed Execution

The head of Tennessee's prison system is resigning following the state's failed execution of death row inmate Christa Pike on Wednesday (The Guardian, South China Morning Post, The Japan Times and others). Governor Bill Lee said that Department of Correction Commissioner Frank Strada will step down this month, without offering further specifics (South China Morning Post, Al Jazeera, Deutsche Welle and others). The state administered two doses of the lethal drug pentobarbital but failed to execute Pike (BBC News, ABC News, CBS News and others); her attorneys said they believed problems with the IV lines caused the drug to flow into her body instead of her bloodstream (ABC News, Al Jazeera, CBS News and others). Pike's lawyers said Friday that she is unconscious and on a ventilator in hospital (The Guardian, The Japan Times, Al Jazeera and others). Lee has ordered an independent review into the attempted execution and halted all executions in the state for the year (BBC News, ABC News, Deutsche Welle and others).

ABC News emphasized the resignation as the first major staff shake-up after what it called a stunning failed execution, noting it was the second since May and that some Republicans had urged a change in leadership. CBS News reported the news in a straightforward tone, highlighting Strada's statement defending the department's adherence to protocol. Deutsche Welle noted witnesses' accounts that Pike was still breathing and moaning after two doses, while The Guardian emphasized the gravity of what it described as a botched execution, quoting attorneys and advocates demanding transparency. BBC News focused on Pike's critical condition and her legal team's response, and The Japan Times centered on her medical condition. Al Jazeera highlighted Strada's Arizona history and the suspension of executions, and France 24 noted the halt of executions. The framing notes describe these terms—"botched execution," "cruel and torturous," "horrific"—as outlets' own language.

The two accounts that differ are each a detail reported by one outlet alone. Only The Guardian reported attorney Randy Spivey's statement that he counted seven needles inserted into Pike's left arm. Only ABC News reported that Pike was cooperative with the execution team, instructing them where to try inserting lines.

## Spain: Protests Over Housing Continue After Parliament Rejects Tenant Relief Measures

Fresh protests over housing flared in Madrid and other Spanish cities on Saturday, a day after parliament narrowly rejected the government's proposed relief measures for tenants (Euronews, BBC News, The Guardian and others). The measures, put forward on Friday by Sánchez's Socialist-led coalition in the form of two decrees, included a ban on evictions until 2030 and rules governing temporary lettings and lettings by room (Deutsche Welle, The Guardian, BBC News). The government introduced the legislation after nationwide protests over the eviction, on a stretcher, of 87-year-old Maricarmen Abascal from a Madrid apartment she had occupied for around seven decades, an event that catalysed long-running anger about runaway housing costs (Euronews, Al Jazeera, BBC News and others; France 24, The Guardian, BBC News). The largest demonstration took place in Madrid, where four columns of protesters filled the capital's main boulevards before converging on Cibeles Square (POLITICO Europe, France 24, BBC News). In Valencia, police fired rubber bullets and tear gas to break up demonstrations (Al Jazeera, Deutsche Welle, The Guardian), after dozens of protesters managed to enter the city's City of Arts and Sciences landmark before being ejected (BBC News, Deutsche Welle, Al Jazeera and others). Spanish media reported that Sánchez might call a snap election for November, with the government watching Saturday's protests closely before deciding (The Guardian, Deutsche Welle, BBC News).

Euronews emphasized the scale and mood of the Madrid protest and linked it to the eviction, using language like "public anger has intensified." POLITICO Europe led with Sánchez weighing a snap election and highlighted turnout estimates. Deutsche Welle focused on the parliamentary rejection and protesters' frustration, while BBC News emphasized "anger in the streets" and the political fallout. The Guardian stressed the protest's scale and political implications, and France 24 framed the protests as a continuation of a "crisis" triggered by the eviction. Al Jazeera described the protests as deepening a "days-long crisis" and noted the general strike call.

The outlets disagreed on where the Valencia clashes occurred: Deutsche Welle reported that dozens of protesters got inside the City of Arts and Sciences before being ejected, while BBC News placed clashes near the arts centre Palau de les Arts Reina Sofía, which was hosting a real estate summit, and France 24 and Al Jazeera both said about 30 protesters entered the City of Arts and Sciences. Madrid turnout estimates varied widely, from 70,000 according to government and police sources to 500,000 according to organizers. Estimates of the rent increase sought from Abascal also differed: The Guardian reported a 230% hike demanded by the investment fund, while France 24 reported 275% according to housing campaigners. Only The Guardian reported that far-right protesters, some performing Nazi salutes, tried to reach the Madrid camp late on Saturday and were blocked by police, with three arrests.

## Kyiv: Russian Strikes Damage Bridge Linking City's Banks

A Russian strike damaged the Northern Bridge across the Dnipro River in Kyiv on Saturday morning, forcing traffic restrictions and disrupting public transport as Russia intensified attacks on infrastructure connecting the capital's eastern and western districts (Brussels Morning, France 24, New York Times and others). Traffic across the bridge from the left bank to the right bank was blocked, according to an official statement (Deutsche Welle, BBC News, Brussels Morning). A total of eight bridges connect Kyiv's western and eastern banks along the Dnipro River (BBC News, POLITICO Europe, Deutsche Welle). Separately, Russia's Foreign Ministry warned foreign diplomats and nationals in Kyiv to leave the city, saying that "individuals are knowingly exposing themselves to mortal danger" (BBC News, France 24, Deutsche Welle).

The New York Times framed the bridge strikes as part of a broad Russian bombing campaign against infrastructure, emphasizing the threat to a vital lifeline for commuters. POLITICO Europe reported the strike as part of Moscow's stepped-up aerial campaign, highlighting the scale of daily commutes and prior drone attacks on the Southern Bridge. Brussels Morning provided a detailed timeline of transport disruptions, with a note that the bridge was damaged, not destroyed. Deutsche Welle led with Ukraine's vow to retaliate on oil refineries, framing the strikes as part of a new Russian strategy to pressure residents to leave cities before winter. BBC News emphasized the unprecedented nature of the attacks and the threat of Kyiv being 'cut in two', while France 24 focused on deepening travel chaos and the third day of disruption.

The outlets disagreed on how many times the Southern Bridge was hit: POLITICO Europe reported it was hit by drones "in recent days" without specifying a count, Deutsche Welle said it had been hit "several times" over the past two days, and BBC News reported it was struck "multiple times" this week. Casualty figures also varied, from no injuries reported by Brussels Morning to two people injured according to Deutsche Welle, BBC News and France 24. The outlets differed on the number of bridges over the Dnipro, with France 24 citing six road bridges while POLITICO Europe, Brussels Morning and Deutsche Welle reported eight. Only Brussels Morning provided a detailed timeline of the Northern Bridge attack, from a drone threat at 6.32am to the traffic closure at 7.39am.

## Latvia: Exit Polls Put PM's Pro-Ukraine Party Ahead in Parliamentary Election

Deutsche Welle led with the exit poll results and Kulbergs' reaction to a "remarkably good" result, highlighting populist gains and living-cost complaints. France 24 focused on the PM's unexpectedly wide lead alongside his coalition partners' stumble, quoting analysts on the reliability of exit polls. ## Trump Urges Senator Cotton to Back Permanent Daylight Saving Time

Donald Trump on Saturday pressured US senator Tom Cotton of Arkansas to support a bill making daylight saving time permanent, urging Americans to call what appeared to be Cotton's personal cellphone number in a post that included a number with an Arkansas area code (The Guardian, NBC News, Al Jazeera and others). Trump wrote on social media that passing the bill would give professional golfers more time on the greens (Al Jazeera, The Guardian, CBS News). Cotton, a member of Republican leadership, reiterated his opposition in a social media post of his own, writing that Congress has enacted permanent daylight saving time multiple times before and repealed it each time once Americans experienced absurdly late winter sunrises (CBS News, The Guardian, NBC News). A Senate bill to make daylight saving time permanent passed in 2022 but stalled in the House (The Guardian, CBS News, NBC News).

CBS News led with the release of the personal cellphone number, framing it as pressure on Cotton and noting it was not the first time Trump had released a senator's personal information. NBC News focused on the sequence of Trump's social media posts, Cotton's public opposition and the bill's history, quoting directly from posts and statements. The Guardian treated the number's publication as a distinct act, adding historical context of Trump's past release of a senator's number. Al Jazeera highlighted Trump ramping up pressure on Senate Republicans and singling out Cotton, quoting another Republican senator calling the measure "dead as disco."

Only The Guardian reported that both The Associated Press and The Guardian called the number without success, reaching a full voicemail box or a message saying the customer was unavailable; Al Jazeera reported that its own call reached an automated message stating the voicemail box was full. Only The Guardian reported Cotton's Senate floor arguments last year about late sunrises, including until 8am or 8.30am in Arkansas, 9am in Seattle, and nearly 9.45am in Williston, North Dakota.

## Brazil: Millions Vote Sunday in Tight Presidential Election

Millions of Brazilians will vote on Sunday in an election dominated by two rivals: the incumbent president, Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, and Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, son of former leader Jair Bolsonaro, whom Lula defeated four years ago (Euronews, Al Jazeera, BBC News and others). Lula is locked in a tight battle against the right-wing senator, 45, whose father, a close ally of Trump, is serving a 27-year sentence for attempting a coup after his failed 2022 re-election bid (Al Jazeera, BBC News, Euronews and others). In a highly polarised Brazil, the candidates' policies and pledges have been overshadowed by a torrent of scandals and personal attacks as they fight for the attention of every undecided voter (Al Jazeera, Euronews, France 24). During the campaign it emerged that Flávio Bolsonaro was under investigation for corruption linked to the funding of the biopic "Dark Horse" about his father, financed by jailed banker Daniel Vorcaro (Al Jazeera, Euronews, France 24). An explosive corruption scandal centred on Vorcaro has raised questions for both campaigns and implicated members of the country's political and economic elite across the political spectrum, while Lula has warned Trump against "meddling" in the election (Euronews, France 24, Al Jazeera).

Euronews led with the final campaign rallies and emphasized the personal attacks and scandals, using charged phrases like "bitter campaign." BBC News framed the story as a question of US election interference, emphasizing Lula's accusations and the broader implications for Brazil's sovereignty. France 24 focused on the election being a "knife-edge" and "nail-biter," highlighting voter quotes and the Trump factor with a tone of high stakes and division. Al Jazeera provided a comprehensive, neutral and analytical explainer covering the candidates, issues, polls and concerns about US intervention.

The accounts differ on poll numbers: Al Jazeera reported a Quaest survey from September showing Lula at 37 percent against Bolsonaro's 33 percent, an AtlasIntel survey showing Lula winning 46 to 43, and a Datafolha poll with Lula at 40 percent and Bolsonaro at 36, while Euronews said the two are neck and neck and neither is set to win outright in Sunday's first round, and France 24 reported a statistical tie for an October 25 runoff. Only Euronews reported that Washington last year slapped 50% tariffs on Brazilian goods, citing among other reasons the trial of Jair Bolsonaro, followed by further tariffs and the designation of Brazil's biggest drug-trafficking gangs as foreign terrorist organisations. ## Hawaii: Holei Sea Arch Collapses in Hawaii Volcanoes National Park

## Bulgaria Receives Remains of Czar Samuel with State Honors

The remains of Czar Samuel, a medieval Bulgarian ruler still regarded as a symbol of Bulgarian national identity, are being returned to Bulgaria with full state honors, roughly 60 years after they were discovered in a Greek border area (Deutsche Welle, The Guardian, Al Jazeera). In return, Greece is set to receive 48 sentimental objects pillaged by Bulgarian troops from churches and monasteries in northern Greece during the First World War (Al Jazeera, Deutsche Welle, The Guardian). Speaking before the handover, Greek Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis said he hoped the ceremony would boost Greece's campaign to repatriate the Parthenon Marbles, 2,500-year-old marble sculptures taken from the Acropolis in Athens and later displayed in the British Museum (The Guardian, Al Jazeera, Deutsche Welle). "The global community is essentially re-evaluating its longstanding stance on protecting cultural heritage," Mitsotakis said, adding that historical heirlooms "cannot be treated as the spoils of war" (Al Jazeera, The Guardian, Deutsche Welle).

Deutsche Welle emphasized the diplomatic and historical significance of the return, focusing on Bulgaria's national identity and ongoing claims by North Macedonia, using phrases like "full state honors" and "battle over history." The Guardian led with the idea that Greece's gesture sets a precedent for repatriation, particularly regarding the Parthenon Marbles, using emotive language such as "priceless relics" and "revered medieval warrior ruler." Al Jazeera focused on the emotional homecoming and the competing claims between Bulgaria and North Macedonia, highlighting the symbolic return after "1,000 years" and the crowds' reactions.

Only The Guardian reported that Bulgaria's prime minister, Rumen Radev, is a former air force officer. Only Al Jazeera quoted Bulgarian citizens at the ceremony, including Martin Nestorov, a 42-year-old software engineer, who said he attended "to welcome Czar Samuel back to Bulgaria, where he belongs," and Tsanka Zlatkova, 65, a retired art historian, who said, "I am here because I am Bulgarian, and a Bulgarian is returning to his homeland."
